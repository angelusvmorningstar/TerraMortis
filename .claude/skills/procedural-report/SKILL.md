# Procedural Report

**A result is a report. It is not a scene.** Its only job is to transmit, plainly and
in-world, what the character learned or what happened. It has no build, no payoff, and
nothing withheld.

## The one-line form

When Angelus asks for a **procedural result**, give **one line**, plain, second person,
diegetic. His own calibration example:

> You learn that Reed has strong presence within the Underworld and your allies seem to
> fear him.

It is still prose. It is just simple prose framed in in-world terms. It is **not** ST
shorthand — `net 2, standing confirmed, no names` is wrong in the other direction. No dice,
no merit names, no success counts, no mechanics of any kind.

Then stop. No preamble, no framing, no offer of next steps.

## The long form

The report section in the player's downtime document is the same register, at whatever
length the facts require. Report what the source reported. Worked examples, all signed off:

> Your underworld allies report that Reed Justice has a strong presence in the Underworld.
> They are afraid of him and will not give you the name of anyone working under him. The
> places where his people are usually found have been emptied in the last few weeks.

> Your police contact finds no file on Reed Justice. No charges, no briefs of evidence, no
> intelligence reports. He tells you that a man that big in the underworld with nothing
> written about him is one of the people who runs this city. He has no names for you.

> Your legal contact gives you two names you did not have. Don Ezzelino Rocio and Conrad
> Sondergaard both have underworld connections. Reed Justice is named as well, but nobody
> will discuss him further.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **State the fact. Imply the feeling.** Never make the player decode the finding from
   clues. Never tell them how their character feels about it. Both at once, always.
2. **No clause that exists to make a sentence land.** If it carries no fact, cut it. This
   is the single highest-yield rule here: reaching for cadence is what invents content.
3. **No drama.** No build, no rhetorical pairs ("You learn X. You learn nothing of Y."),
   no withheld last line, no closing sting.
4. **The report ends on its last fact. No closing summary line.** Do not write "That is the
   whole of it", "Nobody can tell you who he is", or any sentence whose job is to tell the
   player what they did *not* get. That is gloss — me narrating the shape of the answer
   instead of giving it. **The absence speaks for itself: if a name is not in the report, the
   player did not get the name.**
   *The exception is when the absence IS the finding.* "They are afraid of him and will not
   give you the name of anyone working under him" stays, because the refusal is what Aleksei
   learned about Reed. The test: does the sentence carry a fact about the world, or does it
   only summarise what I chose not to say?
5. **No gloss on PC names.** Never "Conrad Sondergaard, the private eye". Every PC has met
   every other PC at Court; the name is the handle. Non-PCs (NPCs, mortals, objects) DO get
   a short tag, because the player cannot place them otherwise.
6. **Answer only what was asked.** Do not volunteer adjacent detail from the target's sheet
   to enrich the answer.
7. **Nothing the source could not know.** A mortal contact reports what that person could
   credibly know. Check it against the mechanics before writing it: if a Hide/Protect
   screened something, it is not in the report; if it did not, do not write as though it did.
8. **No mechanics in voice.** No pools, dots, successes, merit names or rulings.
9. **British English. No em-dashes. Second person, present tense.**

## The failure mode this skill exists to prevent

On 2026-07-14 I wrote a Contacts result using `shortform-composition` and produced, in one
short passage: a PC glossed with the wrong profession ("Conrad Sondergaard, the policeman" —
he is a private eye), an unasked-for Professional Training detail, and a flat contradiction
(that Conrad *hides* his underworld connections, in the same breath as reporting that Yusuf's
contact had named him — when in fact his hide/protect covered Allies (Police) only, which is
exactly why he surfaced).

None of those were independent slips. **The prose pressure invented the content.** Each was a
clause reached for to complete a cadence, then filled with something plausible. That is what
happens when scene craft is pointed at information delivery.

## Which skill to load

- Angelus says **"procedural"**, or the task is an action's RESULT → **this skill**.
- Angelus says **"narrative"**, or the task is a Story Moment, a touchstone vignette, a POV
  scene, a rumour, an invasion or ambush scene → **`shortform-composition`**.

Scenes get craft. Results get reported.
